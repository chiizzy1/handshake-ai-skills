# Cheat Sheet

Source: the Cheat Sheet section of `HANDSHAKE-AI/ads-creative/vs-1788462573-260903-ads-creative-i2i-relevance.md`,
reproduced without edits.

The question: does the generated ad still sell what the original ad was selling? Pass or Fail. If Fail, check every reason that applies.

Before you judge, read the original ad. Three things:

The problem. What problem is this business solving? Usually in the headline.
The payoff. What does the buyer get? Almost always: look better, feel better, save money, make money, or save time.
The support. What else is doing sales work? Selling points, prices, demonstrations, partnerships.
Now open the generated ad. Did all three survive? If the problem or the payoff is gone, it is a Fail, however good the image looks.

Ad type. Hero sells a physical product the customer takes home. Non-Hero sells a service, access, an experience, or a digital product. Apps, games, and software are Non-Hero. The type is pre-selected; correct it if it is wrong.

Not your questions

Is it different from the original? It is supposed to be. A big change is not a Fail by itself.
Is it good advertising? The advertiser already decided that. You check whether their decisions survived.
Is it well made? Warped hands and garbled type belong to other tasks. Beautiful and irrelevant can both be true.
One exception: any change to the product itself (color, shape, count, markings) is a relevance problem. It belongs under product preservation, never to "rendering quality".

How to look: two images side by side at normal size. A difference you can only find by zooming in is not a Fail.

Signals that something must survive: a name printed under a person, two brands in one image, a price printed larger than the headline.

Never invented: nothing about the advertiser can begin in the output. An invented room, view, amenity, interface, or product is false advertising, even when it looks great.
