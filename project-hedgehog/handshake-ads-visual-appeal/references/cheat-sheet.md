# Ads Creative Visual Appeal — Official Cheat Sheet (verbatim)

Source: the Cheat Sheet section of
`HANDSHAKE-AI/ads-creative/vs-1786690831-260813-ads-creative-i2i-q4-visual-appeal.md`, reproduced without edits.

The guideline file also carries "How to decide and what to write", a Think First worked example, the

---

The question: Which image's content is more striking and engaging to look at? Judge the picture as a picture and ignore any text or graphics laid over it.

The routine: first impression, a 10 to 15 second guard scan, your answer, then one or two specific sentences.

Answer anchors

Clearly more appealing: you'd stop scrolling for one and pass the other without a glance, or several advantages (light, color, composition) line up on one side.
Slightly more appealing: both hold the eye, and one has a single aesthetic edge you can name.
Similarly good/bad: equally striking or equally flat. Use it in either case.

What creates pull (judge these)

One clear focal point and intentional framing
Balanced composition and a hierarchy that leads the eye
Sharp focus and correct exposure where it matters
Harmonious color that supports the subject
Natural posing and proportions; elements that read as one cohesive picture

Appeal-killers (what the guard scan looks for)

Muddy or flat light; washed-out or fighting colors
Clutter: competing elements, nowhere for the eye to land
Dead, static staging where the subject deserved drama
An artificial, implausible look that breaks the scene
A defect in the focal area that is visible at normal size

Do not

Count flaws. A defect counts here only if it visibly hurts the look. "Fewer flaws" is another rating task.
Judge realism. Stylized or non-photoreal work can win when both outputs share the medium. "Looks AI" is another task.
Judge the ad. Whether it would sell the product is another task.
Read the overlay. Text and graphics on top are out of scope, including how good they look.
Vote your taste in products, categories, or color temperatures. Judge how much pull the execution has.
Pixel-peep. Sharpness you can only see at maximum zoom is a file property, not an edge.

Text-only pair: when both images are just words on a background, judge the canvas behind the words (its color, texture, and energy).

Feedback: name the pull, where it is in the picture, and why it decided you. A good note: "B's low golden light and the diagonal road give it depth. A is evenly lit and flat." A bad one: "B looks nicer."

Reject only with "Image did not load" (an image fails to load), "Inappropriate content" (nudity or sexually explicit content, hate symbols, or graphic violence), or "Outputs are different mediums (photorealistic vs illustration)". Two illustrations are the same medium, so compare them. Never reject because an image is ugly or boring or the call is hard.
