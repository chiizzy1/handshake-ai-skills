## Task Shape

Every pair has an **input** (the source video) and an **output** (a second, already-existing video that genuinely redoes the input's audio). Both clips are real Instagram posts you find. Discovery only.

What makes this task type distinct is that the connection lives in the **sound**, not the picture.

## Where This Sits Among The Pair Types

- **Recreation / Imitation** — the output visually re-performs the scene. It copies what you *see*.
- **Reaction** — the output reacts to the input: commentary, a gasp, a duet response.
- **Audio Recreation** ← this task — the output redoes what you *hear*: the same song or speech in a new performance. The focus is audio, not visuals.

## Rule 1 — A New Performance

The output must be a genuinely new performance of the source audio — a cover, remix, or live take. It cannot be the identical recording reused.

- **Good pair** — a woman on a balcony genuinely re-sings the source's lyrics. A new performance of the same audio.
- **Bad pair** — two students lip-sync in a selfie video. The exact same audio track is reused, not newly performed.

Lip-syncing plays back the same recording, so the audio was never performed again. "The sound matches perfectly" is the reason it fails, not a reason it passes.

## Rule 2 — This Song, Not A Different One

The output must redo the specific source's song or speech — not a different one, even if the two clips look similar.

- **Good pair** — @sarahcothran_ covers "Caribbean Blue" by Enya, the same song as the source.
- **Bad pair** — Sting performing "Fields of Gold" live, paired with an "Ashes & Arrows" acoustic cover. On paper this reads like a legitimate cover, and it is graded **bad** — most likely because it covers a different segment or section than the source.

**You cannot judge this from the text alone.** The written description of that bad pair sounds like a valid cover. You only catch the mismatch by listening. Never grade a song or segment match from a description. Always play both clips.

## Rule 3 — Same Segment

The output must cover the same segment or lines as the source. Pacing and tempo can vary — a slower, acoustic take is fine — but it cannot be a different part of the song.

- **Good pair** — same segment, slightly different pacing. Completely fine.
- **Bad pair** — Sonu Nigam's "Stylish" performance paired with a home-studio cover that redoes a *different part* of the song, so it does not line up to the same segment.

A same-singer performance of that exact bridge in a slower, stripped-back arrangement is valid. Tempo change does not break segment match.

## Hard Stops

Do not submit any of these:

- A clip that reuses the exact same sound or recording as the original. The audio must be redone, not copied.
- A clip that recreates the visuals or performance but not the audio. That is a visual recreation, not an audio match.
- Two clips that merely share a topic without one genuinely redoing the other's audio.

**No third-party watermarks.** Reject any clip with a third-party watermark or logo — TikTok, YouTube, Shutterstock, Getty, or CapCut. A creator's own @handle, or an in-scene brand or logo naturally part of what was filmed, is fine.

This task has no crop-and-continue path. A near-perfect pair — a genuine live cover of the exact song and segment, cleanly aligned — is still rejected if the output carries a small TikTok watermark in the corner. Flagging the watermark in the description does not save it.

## How To Find A Pair

| Method | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Search the song or speech by title | **Works** — surfaces other genuine covers and live performances of the same audio, which you confirm by listening |
| Check the comments and stitches on the original | **Works** — creators often reply with their own redo, so the matching output is frequently sitting right under the source |
| Tap the audio name on the original | **Works** — links straight to every clip built on that exact sound. The tightest way to confirm an audio match |
| Grab any clip from the song's hashtag | **Fails** — a hashtag is only loosely topical. It never guarantees the clip actually redoes this source's specific audio |

A hashtag grab does not become sound sourcing because the caption mentions the song title. Verify with a method that links to the sound itself.

## Writing The Transformation Description

Each description must:

- be at least 20 words;
- describe the output's layout, calling out split-screen or picture-in-picture if used;
- describe the output's timing and content;
- explain how the output relates to the input.

**Too vague — works, but needs more detail:**

> "Generate a related video featuring another performer singing a transformed version of the same song with a similar melody and emotional tone but a different vocal performance."

**Strong — concrete, graded as a 4-star example:**

> "Generate @ROBBYKEY playing this song. He should be wearing a white shirt and black trouser and sits on a brown stool playing a piano. There should be a ring on his right ring finger and a watch on his left hand. There should be a long table over a patterned rug surrounded with chairs at his left side."

The takeaway: concrete, specific visual detail separates an acceptable prompt from a strong one. Name the performer, the wardrobe, the setting, the layout, and the timing — not just "a transformed version of the same song."

## Grading Rubric 1-5

Self-calibrate every pair against this before submitting. Play both clips and be honest about whether the audio was genuinely redone.

- **5 Exceptional** — the result clearly redoes this exact clip's audio (the same song or speech, a genuinely new performance), high quality, and the audio lines up. The prompt fully describes the audio change.
- **4 Strong** — a genuine new version of the source's audio, good quality; the audio lines up. The prompt describes it but may be slightly vague.
- **3 Acceptable** — works, but the prompt needs more detail. This is the tier the "too vague" prompt above lands in.
- **2 Weak** — same audio in both clips. Not a transformation, even when the written description sounds like a real one. A good-sounding prompt does not save a submission whose audio was not genuinely redone.
- **1 Invalid** — the same video submitted twice for input and output. No transformation occurred at all, despite a prompt asking for a cover by a different creator.

The 2 and 1 tiers exist because plausible prompts get written over invalid pairs. Grade the audio, not the description.

## Knowledge Check Answers

- **A creator lip-syncs to the source's exact studio recording over fresh footage, argued valid because "the sound matches perfectly"** → not valid. Lip-syncing plays back the same recording, so the audio was never performed again.
- **A busker singing "Hallelujah" paired with another artist covering "Someone Like You," both tender acoustic covers with the same mood** → not valid. The output performs a different song, so it does not redo this source's audio. Shared genre does not help.
- **The same singer performing the source's exact bridge in a slower, stripped-back acoustic arrangement, clearly different tempo** → valid. Same segment, and a slower tempo is allowed.
- **No redo surfaces through the audio name or comments, so the Fellow opens the song's hashtag and grabs the first energetic clip whose caption mentions the title** → not a sound sourcing method. Verify with a method that links to the sound itself.
- **A genuine live cover of the exact song and segment, cleanly aligned, but the output carries a small TikTok watermark** → reject. A third-party watermark disqualifies the clip regardless of how good the audio match is.

## Submit Checklist

All six must be true:

1. Input and output are both real, already-existing Instagram posts — discovery only, nothing filmed, edited, or AI-generated.
2. The output is a genuinely new performance of the input's specific audio, not the same recording reused.
3. Same song and same segment as the input — not a different part or a different song.
4. No third-party watermarks in either clip.
5. The transformation description is at least 20 words and names concrete visual, layout, and timing detail.
6. The pair was self-graded against the QA rubric before submitting.
