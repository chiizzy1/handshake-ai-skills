# Critical Components, Step-by-Step, and Justification

From the onboarding documentation. **These three sections are what the rubric is
built from**, alongside the golden response itself.

> "The rubric for the task will be built off of the golden response that you are
> offering as being true but is reliant on the additional information of the
> critical components and the step-by-step solution."

## Critical components

The **indispensable findings that justify the final recommendation**. They become
the backbone of the rubric used to score model responses.

> **At least four. Generally no more than ten.**

More components do not make a stronger recommendation, and can be
counterproductive — **if one component negates another you have called critical,
the task works against itself.**

A strong critical component does one of these:

- correctly indicates the analysis and identifies the resolution
- provides a **metric definition**
- determines the **inclusion and exclusion criteria** for the data analysed
- articulates a **statistical gate that must be crossed**
- provides **justification for the final rankings** behind the recommendation

Every one is specific, numeric where a number decides it, and falsifiable against
a shipped file. See the strong/weak table in the parent skill.

## Step-by-step solution

The numbered process taking someone **from the raw data to the final
recommendation**.

Each step must identify:

- the **order** it is performed in
- the **evidence being examined**
- the **finding reported** from that analytical step

> **Each step as granular as possible — only one action or one specific step**,
> with enough detail for someone to review your work.

The path must be detailed enough that **anyone can follow the process and produce
the same recommendation**, in a logical sequence that justifies the recommendation
as the only correct answer reachable.

## Justification

Where you argue the answer **against the alternatives**.

- What aspects of the data and methods ensure your answer is the correct one
- Refer back to the critical components and the step-by-step
- Pull in the **assumptions** relied on throughout the analysis
- Offer the evidence supporting your answer **and not the alternative**
- Indicate where a flaw in analysis happens when choosing the alternative, the
  assumption needed to support it, or **the stump the alternative fell for**

> Written to convince a **skeptical reviewer**, in professional language.

## The final recommendation statement

> **Plain, concise, a single unambiguous decision. Deterministic and gradable.**
>
> **It must not include any rationales or justifications.**

Those live in the critical components, the step-by-step, and the deliverables —
never in the recommendation statement itself. A recommendation carrying its own
reasoning is a common defect.
